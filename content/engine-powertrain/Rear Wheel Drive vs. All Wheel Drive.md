# Rear Wheel Drive vs. All Wheel Drive

The drivetrain of a car determines how the engine's power gets to the wheels. Two common setups found in performance cars are rear wheel drive and all wheel drive. Both can provide good performance, but they have different characteristics that affect acceleration, handling, and how the car feels to drive.

## Rear Wheel Drive

In a rear wheel drive car, the engine sends power to the rear wheels. This layout is common in sports cars because it can provide a balanced driving experience and allow the front tires to focus more on steering.

Rear wheel drive can also make it easier for a driver to control how the car behaves when accelerating through a corner. However, traction can become more difficult when the road is wet or when the car has a lot of power.

## All Wheel Drive

All wheel drive sends power to all four wheels. The system can distribute power between the front and rear wheels depending on driving conditions and the design of the vehicle. Having power going to four wheels can help a car put its power down more effectively, especially during hard acceleration.

All wheel drive is often useful for high powered performance cars because it can help reduce wheelspin. The tradeoff is that all wheel drive systems can add weight and mechanical complexity to a vehicle.

## Choosing Between Them

The drivetrain affects more than just acceleration. It can change how a car handles, how much traction it has, and how the driver experiences the vehicle. Rear wheel drive can provide a more traditional sports car feel, while all wheel drive can provide additional traction and confidence when putting power to the road.

When comparing performance cars, the drivetrain should be considered along with the engine, transmission, weight, tires, and suspension. These systems all work together to determine how the car performs.
