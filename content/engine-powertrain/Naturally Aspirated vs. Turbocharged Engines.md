# Naturally Aspirated vs. Turbocharged Engines

When comparing performance cars, one of the biggest differences between engines is whether they are naturally aspirated or turbocharged. Both setups can produce strong performance, but they deliver power in different ways. This difference can also affect how the engine feels when driving.

## Naturally Aspirated Engines

A naturally aspirated engine does not use a turbocharger or supercharger to force extra air into the engine. Air enters the engine through normal atmospheric pressure. As the engine speed increases, the engine can continue to build power toward higher RPMs.

One characteristic of a naturally aspirated engine is the way the power delivery can feel predictable and direct. Some performance cars use this type of engine because it can provide a strong connection between throttle input and engine response. The Porsche 911 GT3 is one example of a performance car that uses a naturally aspirated engine.

## Turbocharged Engines

A turbocharged engine uses exhaust gases to spin a turbine, which is connected to a compressor. The compressor pushes more air into the engine. More air allows the engine to burn more fuel and produce additional power.

Turbocharging can help a smaller engine produce a large amount of power and torque. Modern turbocharged engines are also designed to reduce the delay that used to be common with older turbo systems. This can make the power delivery feel much more responsive.
