# Horsepower and Torque

Horsepower and torque are two numbers that are often used when talking about how much power a car has. They are related, but they describe different things. Understanding the difference helps when comparing performance cars and their engines.

## Horsepower

Horsepower is a measurement of how quickly an engine can do work. It is commonly used to describe the overall power an engine can produce. A car with more horsepower has the potential to reach higher speeds and accelerate strongly, especially when the engine can keep producing power at higher RPMs.

Horsepower by itself does not tell the whole story, though. The weight of the car, transmission, gearing, and drivetrain can all affect how that power is actually put to the road.

## Torque

Torque describes the twisting force produced by an engine. It can be felt when a car starts moving or when it accelerates from a lower speed. Engines that produce a lot of torque at lower RPMs can feel strong when accelerating without needing to rev very high.

Different types of engines can deliver torque in different ways. Turbocharged engines, for example, can produce strong torque at relatively low RPMs, while some naturally aspirated engines are designed to build power as the engine speed increases.

## How They Work Together

Horsepower and torque work together to determine how an engine delivers its performance. Torque provides the force to turn the wheels, while horsepower takes into account how quickly that work can be done. This is why looking at both numbers gives a better idea of an engine's characteristics than looking at only one.

When comparing performance cars, it is also useful to consider the entire vehicle instead of focusing only on horsepower or torque. Weight, gearing, traction, and the way the engine delivers its power can make a major difference in how a car actually feels on the road.
