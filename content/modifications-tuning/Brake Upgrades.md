# Brake Upgrades

Brakes are an important part of any performance car because more speed also means the car needs to be able to slow down effectively. Brake upgrades can improve stopping performance and help the braking system handle repeated hard use.

## Brake Pads

Brake pads press against the brake rotors to create friction and slow the vehicle. Performance brake pads can be designed to handle higher temperatures and harder driving than standard pads.

Different brake pads can also have different characteristics. Some are designed for everyday driving, while others are made for track use. A pad that works well on a track may not always be the best choice for a daily driven car.

## Rotors and Calipers

Brake rotors are the discs that the brake pads press against. Larger or differently designed rotors can provide more surface area and help manage heat. Calipers hold the brake pads and apply pressure to the rotors.

Performance cars may use larger calipers with multiple pistons. This can provide more consistent pressure against the brake pads and improve the overall braking system.

## Brake Cooling

Heat is one of the biggest concerns during repeated hard braking. As the brakes are used, friction creates heat. If the system gets too hot, braking performance can decrease.

Some performance cars use brake ducts or other methods to direct air toward the brakes. Keeping temperatures under control is especially important during track driving.

## Choosing Brake Upgrades

A brake upgrade should match the way the vehicle is being used. A daily driven performance car may benefit from quality pads, rotors, and fresh brake fluid without needing a full racing brake system.

Brakes also work together with the tires and suspension. Even a powerful braking system still depends on the tires having enough grip to slow the car effectively. For that reason, improving one part of the braking system should be considered as part of the vehicle's overall setup.
