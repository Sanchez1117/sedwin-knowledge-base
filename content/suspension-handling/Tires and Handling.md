# Tires and Handling

Tires are one of the most important parts of a performance car because they are the only part of the vehicle that actually touches the road. The engine can produce a lot of power and the suspension can be well designed, but the car still depends on its tires to put that performance to the ground.

## Grip and Traction

Tire grip affects how well a car can accelerate, turn, and brake. Performance tires are designed to provide more grip than many standard tires, especially during harder driving. The amount of grip can change depending on the tire compound, temperature, road surface, and weather.

Traction is especially important for high powered cars. If the tires cannot handle the amount of power being produced, the wheels can spin instead of moving the car forward efficiently.

## Tire Size and Design

The size and design of a tire can also affect handling. Wider tires can provide more contact with the road, although width is not the only factor that determines grip. The construction and rubber compound of the tire are also important.

Performance cars may use different tire sizes on the front and rear. This can be part of the vehicle's overall handling design and can help provide the amount of grip needed at each end of the car.

## Tires and Everyday Driving

Performance tires can improve handling, but there can be tradeoffs. Some high performance tires are designed mainly for dry conditions and may not work as well in cold or wet weather. They can also wear faster than tires designed for normal daily driving.

Tire choice should match how the car is being used. A car driven mainly on public roads may need a different tire than one that regularly goes to a track.

Tires work closely with the suspension, brakes, steering, and drivetrain. Because of this, changing the tires can have a noticeable effect on how a performance car feels and responds.
